    <details class="project-entry">
      <summary>
        <span class="project-name">🤖 Blame as Sensemaking: A Computational Mapping of Target Framings Across Online AI Discourse</span>
        <span class="project-tag">CHI 2026</span>
        <span class="project-toggle project-toggle--open">▷ expand</span>
        <span class="project-toggle project-toggle--closed">▽ expanded</span>
      </summary>
      <div class="project-body">
        <p>The increased human-likeness of AI agents has intensified discussions on the perceived moral agency of AI, yet insufficient attention has been paid to the everyday language used to describe its actions. Addressing this gap, this study computationally analyzes anthropomorphic blame language in AI-related discussions on Reddit. Focusing on blame situations in the two largest AI-related subreddits, we explore the semantic similarities across blame language targeting AI, human, organizational, and societal actors.</p>
        <p>Analysis of post-level embeddings reveals high overall semantic similarity across targets, with distinctive topical profiles. Blame directed at AI agents aligns closest to society-level blaming across topical anchors, while moral action anchors reveal structures unique to AI blaming. These findings suggest that AI is recognized as blameworthy, but the tone and style of its linguistic description are largely shared with other actors. We highlight the implications of these patterns in the public sensemaking of responsible AI design and governance.</p>

    <details class="project-entry">
      <summary>
        <span class="project-name">🔮 Futuring the Quasi-Human: Contested Imaginaries of AI-Human Boundaries Across Institutional and Vernacular Discourse</span>
        <span class="project-toggle project-toggle--open">▷ expand</span>
        <span class="project-toggle project-toggle--closed">▽ expanded</span>
      </summary>
      <div class="project-body">
        <p>The boundaries between human and artificial intelligence are not fixed technical facts but are actively imagined through everyday language. While studies of sociotechnical imaginaries have often examined institutionally stabilized visions, such as national AI strategies and policy reports, everyday imaginaries constructed within social media discourse remain undertheorized as sites where AI-human boundaries are contested before they become institutionally stabilized.</p>
        <p>Employing a mixed methodology that integrates structural topic modeling, comparative discourse analysis, and interpretive scenario building, the study systematically compares how institutional and vernacular groups make sense of AI’s seemingly immaterial quasi-human capacities, including reasoning, creativity, judgment, empathy, and moral responsibility.</p>
